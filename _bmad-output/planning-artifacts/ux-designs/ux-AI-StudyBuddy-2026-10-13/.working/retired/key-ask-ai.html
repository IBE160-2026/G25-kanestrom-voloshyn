What is the difference between mitosis and meiosis?

Study Buddy

Good question, they look alike at first. Based on your lecture and notes, the key differences are:

  • Purpose. Mitosis makes new body cells for growth and repair. Meiosis makes gametes (sperm and egg cells).
  • Number of divisions. Mitosis divides once. Meiosis divides twice, meiosis I and meiosis II.
  • Result. Mitosis gives two diploid cells identical to the parent. Meiosis gives four haploid cells, each genetically different.
  • Homologous chromosomes. They pair up and swap segments (crossing over) only in meiosis I.

So is crossing over the reason the four cells end up different?

Study Buddy

It's one of two reasons your slides give. Crossing over in prophase I swaps segments between homologous chromosomes, so each chromatid becomes a new mix of maternal and paternal genes.

The other is independent assortment in metaphase I: each homologous pair lines up in a random orientation, so which chromosome goes to which pole is down to chance. Together they're why no two gametes are quite alike.

What happens to the nuclear envelope during prophase?

Study Buddy

In your lecture, prophase is when the nuclear envelope starts to break down, so the spindle fibres can reach the chromosomes. It re-forms around each new set of chromosomes in telophase.
