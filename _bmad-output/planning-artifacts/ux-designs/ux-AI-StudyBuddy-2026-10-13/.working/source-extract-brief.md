# Source Extract: Brief → UX Signal (AI Study Buddy)

## 1. Brand Voice / Tone Language
- "upload once, study many ways, review what you got wrong" — the core-loop tagline; plain, functional, sequential phrasing (not aspirational/hype).
- "turn that material into something they can actually study *from*" — emphasis on usefulness over novelty.
- "a workflow a student can pick up with no explanation" — tone should read as self-evident, not clever.
- "Version 1 is intentionally narrow: six tightly integrated features and nothing more" — modest, restrained framing.
- App description is matter-of-fact: "no install, just upload and go."

## 2. Differentiation Stance
- "This brief makes no claim of category novelty."
- "Version 1's differentiation is in simplicity and integration, not feature count: one uploaded project flows directly into Quiz, Flashcards, Summarize, or Ask AI, and Mistake Review is a first-class, always-connected part of that same project rather than a bolted-on extra."
- "The goal is a workflow a student can pick up with no explanation, not a longer list of capabilities than the competition."
→ Implication: visual identity should read calm, uncluttered, integrated — not flashy or novelty-driven.

## 3. Primary User (for tone calibration)
"The primary user is a university or college student who regularly studies from digital course material — lecture slides, PDFs, notes, and textbook chapters — and wants to turn that material into active study practice without spending extra time building it by hand."

## 4. Desktop-Web-Only Constraint
- "AI Study Buddy is a desktop web app" (Executive Summary).
- "It is a desktop web app: no install, just upload and go." (Solution)
- Scope: "delivered as a desktop web app" (explicitly listed as the V1 delivery form factor; no mobile app or responsive-mobile commitment stated).

## 5. Out of Scope (UX-relevant)
Explicitly excluded from V1: "detailed progress analytics, spaced repetition, advanced adaptive learning, cross-document knowledge linking, advanced study recommendations, and full exam planning."
Also stated directly: "no gamification, no adaptive personalization, no cross-course intelligence."
→ Implication: no badges, streaks, points, confetti, progress-analytics dashboards, or personalization/recommendation UI patterns — component library should stay functional/restrained, not game-like.
