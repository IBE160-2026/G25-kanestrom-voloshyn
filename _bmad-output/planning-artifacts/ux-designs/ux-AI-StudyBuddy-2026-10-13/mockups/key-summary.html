Cell division: mitosis and meiosis

The cell cycle

A dividing cell spends most of its time in interphase: it grows (G1), copies its DNA (S phase), and prepares to divide (G2). Division itself, the M phase, comes last. Because DNA is copied in S phase, each chromosome enters division as two identical sister chromatids.

lecture-04-cell-division.pptx · slide 4lecture-04-cell-division.pptx · slide 6

Mitosis

Mitosis makes new body cells for growth and repair. It has one division in four stages:

  • Prophase: chromosomes condense and the spindle forms.
  • Metaphase: chromosomes line up at the middle of the cell.
  • Anaphase: sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles.
  • Telophase: new nuclei form at each pole.

The result is two diploid cells, genetically identical to the parent.

lecture-04-cell-division.pptx · slide 8lecture-04-cell-division.pptx · slide 10lecture-04-cell-division.pptx · slide 12

Cytokinesis

After telophase the cytoplasm divides. In animal cells a cleavage furrow pinches the cell in two; in plant cells a cell plate forms between the new nuclei.

lecture-04-cell-division.pptx · slide 13

Meiosis

Meiosis makes gametes (sperm and egg cells). It has two divisions. Meiosis I separates homologous chromosomes, halving the chromosome number. Meiosis II then separates sister chromatids, much like mitosis.

Two events make every gamete different: crossing over in prophase I swaps segments between homologous chromosomes, and independent assortment in metaphase I lines each pair up in a random orientation.

lecture-04-cell-division.pptx · slide 16lecture-04-cell-division.pptx · slide 19lecture-04-cell-division.pptx · slide 21

Mitosis vs meiosis

  • Purpose: mitosis for growth and repair; meiosis for gametes.
  • Divisions: one in mitosis; two in meiosis.
  • Result: two identical diploid cells from mitosis; four genetically different haploid cells from meiosis.
